01 · Hypothesis · Synthetic + Living

Active stability underwater · the control computer

Founder note · Rua · avionics systems · F-16

The F-16 Fighting Falcon is purposefully agile. It behaves more like a dart than a glider. A human cannot hold a precise trajectory without the flight control computer. Relaxed static stability opens the design space; the computer makes that space flyable.

Core claim (working)

EvoMarine’s first prototype should treat attitude and path control the way high-performance airframes treat flight control: a dedicated control computer that holds quiet trajectories when the hull alone is not enough · especially hover, slow station-keeping, and low-disturbance loiter near marine life.

Stability lives in software · the avionics pattern. High-rate sensing and computer authority free the body to favor efficiency, compliance, and quiet.

What makes the F-16 flight control system unique

Relaxed static stability Airframe designed to be highly responsive. The computer holds the trajectory humans cannot hold alone.
Full fly-by-wire Continuous surface drive from the flight control computer · pilot sets rate and g demand.
Quadruplex redundancy Four independent channels · graceful degradation if one fails.
High-rate sensing Accelerometers, rate gyros, air data · continuous correction at high rate.

Transfer map · F-16 pedigree

Air · F-16 Water · Path A (hypothesis)
Agile airframe · multi-axis rates · fly-by-wire Soft-stable or under-damped hull at loiter regimes
Pilot sets demand · computer holds envelope Autonomy or operator sets demand · loop holds quiet hover and slow transit
Flight control computer Underwater control computer · IMU, depth, optional DVL

Control path

  1. Solid attitude and depth loops (PID-class) with strong state estimation
  2. Middle / outer hold · depth and station-keeping · “hold here quietly” before long transit
  3. Model-based control (LTV-MPC class) when coupling and underactuation need it · short horizon, real-time solver on the vehicle

Compute split

A lean stability computer · separate from the observation payload computer. Fly-by-wire pedigree, drone flight stacks, and field hydrobatic AUVs all inform the loop.

02 · Everyday proof · Synthetic lane

What drones already prove

Consumer multirotor · flight stacks · open source

A typical multirotor is not passively stable. Tip it and it wants to keep tipping. Four independent motors, coupled axes, fast dynamics. No human can juggle thrust on each prop in real time the way the vehicle needs.

Layered stack

Inner: attitude · Middle: velocity/position · Outer: “go there / hold here.”

EvoForm diagram

A multirotor tips and keeps tipping without the loop. The pilot never balances four motors. The pilot commands intent. The same layered pattern · retuned for water · is the practical path for EvoMarine quiet dwell.

Military fly-by-wire is heavy because of certification and combat envelopes. Drone controllers are light because the mission tolerates simpler redundancy. Open stacks (PX4, ArduPilot, many custom loops) show this is mature engineering, not exotic research.

Why this is buildable now

Drone flight stacks prove layered stability at low mass and cost · hydrobatic AUVs prove underwater attitude/depth authority with trim and buoyancy · leatherback ridge and stroke–glide results give hull and energy cues with published trails · Prototype 1 combines those for an observation mission and hours-class quiet hold.

Drones prove the control pattern at everyday weight. The leatherback proves the body language of efficient presence. EvoMarine joins them underwater. Living teacher →

04 · Architecture direction

Hull · energy · actuation

Hull Leatherback proportions · planform and longitudinal ridge cues with real hydrodynamic basis
Volume Sized for energy, dual compute, buoyancy, insulation, robotics, and observation payload · bulkier first systems welcome · refine after learning
Motion energy Intermittent thrust with long coast · turtle stroke–glide pattern (~30% effort / ~70% glide in lab biomechanics)
Actuation Hybrid · buoyancy for depth · trim mass for pitch · sparse thrust and/or few controlled foils
Skin language Continuous fairing · ridge geometry as function

This is a craft that stores calm in its geometry and spends energy in short, deliberate windows.

08 · Horizon

A quieter design shift

Much of the last century’s underwater craft optimized for power, depth, or survey speed. Another path is opening: platforms that stay longer by disturbing less · that borrow stability from computation · that take hull language from animals that already cross oceans on sparse effort.

EvoMarine is one working sketch of that shift: performance rooted in living systems, held by modern control, aimed at presence rather than spectacle.
