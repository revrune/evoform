01 · Hypothesis · Synthetic + Living

Active stability underwater · the control computer

Founder note · Rua · avionics systems · F-16

The F-16 Fighting Falcon is purposefully agile. It behaves more like a dart than a glider. A human cannot hold a precise trajectory without the flight control computer. Relaxed static stability opens the design space; the computer makes that space flyable.

Core claim (working)

EvoMarine’s first prototype should treat attitude and path control the way high-performance airframes treat flight control: a dedicated control computer that holds quiet trajectories when the hull alone is not enough · especially hover, slow station-keeping, and low-disturbance loiter near marine life.

Stability lives in software · the avionics pattern. High-rate sensing and computer authority free the body to favor efficiency, compliance, and quiet.

Why this matters for Path A

  • Quiet authority. Soft, coordinated corrections can hold position without continuous hard thrust.
  • Co-presence. Small, frequent surface and thruster inputs support listening and watching.
  • Hydrophone-first layout. The platform can hold still or crawl while sensors stay useful.
  • Form + computer together. Leatherback planform and ridge language lighten the control burden; the computer closes the loop. Each strengthens the other.

Transfer map

Air · F-16 Water · Path A (hypothesis)
Agile airframe · multi-axis rates · fly-by-wire Soft-stable or under-damped hull at loiter regimes
Pilot commands intent · computer holds envelope Operator or autonomy commands intent · loop holds quiet hover and slow transit
Flight control computer Underwater control computer · IMU, depth, optional DVL

Control path

  1. Solid attitude and depth loops (PID-class) with strong state estimation
  2. Model-based control (LTV-MPC class) when coupling and underactuation need it · periodic linearization, short horizon, real-time solver on the vehicle

Compute split

A lean stability computer · separate from the observation payload computer. Lessons from fly-by-wire aircraft and field hydrobatic AUVs (trim, buoyancy, model-based control) inform the loop.

Horizon

The same shift that transformed aviation · from pure passive stability to computer-augmented design · can open a quieter class of underwater platforms: vehicles that stay present without dominating the scene.

02 · Living · Dermochelys coriacea

Leatherback as form teacher

Evolution already solved migration-scale efficiency, pressure-tolerant volume, continuous skin language, ridge geometry, and hydrofoil-style propulsion at large size. We transfer those mechanisms into manufactured form. The closer the performance, the more the craft will naturally share planform with the animal. Resemblance follows performance.

What we learn

Rear planform

Keels, trailing silhouette, flipper geometry as control-surface teachers · machine joints guided by living kinematics.

Adult scale

~3 m class animal as a volume teacher. Plate size and joints are re-optimized for the platform we build.

Surface language

Long body, ridge spine, low-disturbance silhouette in open water.

Mass and presence

First systems may be mass-first: batteries, insulation, electronics, robotic fixtures. Water does not punish weight the way air does. Specialized boat or crane deploy is fine for premium research. Refine, then focus.

Zoom out

Across oceans, forests, and skies, the strongest designs often begin as careful readings of living systems. EvoMarine sits in that lineage: performance first, form as the visible record of what works.

03 · Architecture direction

Hull · energy · actuation

Layered direction for the craft. Complements the first-hull transfer stack below.

Hull Leatherback proportions · planform and longitudinal ridge cues with real hydrodynamic basis
Volume Size chosen for energy, dual compute, buoyancy, insulation, robotics, and observation payload · bulkier first systems welcome · refine after learning
Motion energy Intermittent thrust with long coast · turtle stroke–glide pattern (~30% effort / ~70% glide in lab biomechanics)
Actuation Hybrid · buoyancy for depth · trim mass for pitch · sparse thrust and/or few controlled foils
Skin language Continuous fairing · ridge geometry as function

This is a craft that stores calm in its geometry and spends energy in short, deliberate windows.

05 · Prior work · trial one

We build on

  • Leatherback ridges improve lift-to-drag at low angle of attack in published glider CFD
  • Turtle propulsion studies show thrust concentrated in a minority of the cycle, with mass and low drag carrying the glide
  • Hydrobatic research AUVs already demonstrate computer-owned pitch, depth, and agile maneuvers using buoyancy, moving mass, and model predictive control in the field

Open lane. Observation hull sized for real energy and payload · hybrid buoyancy and sparse thrust · avionics-style authority · quiet presence for sensing.

Trial one · success frame

Multi-hour smooth depth and attitude hold · calm transit with long coast phases · sensors usable throughout. Depth band and endurance numbers lock with scale.

Horizon

A quieter design shift

Much of the last century’s underwater craft optimized for power, depth, or survey speed. Another path is opening: platforms that stay longer by disturbing less · that borrow stability from computation · that take hull language from animals that already cross oceans on sparse effort.

EvoMarine is one working sketch of that shift. A coherent place to think, build, and invite collaborators who want the same standard: performance rooted in living systems, held by modern control, aimed at presence rather than spectacle.
