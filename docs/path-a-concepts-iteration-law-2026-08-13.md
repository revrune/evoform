### 3. Form from nature

Engineer the teacher’s **shape and motion** into something we can build and fly with a control computer.

| Steal | Leave |
|-------|--------|
| Leatherback teardrop + packed volume | Costume head, neck, eyes |
| Flipper-style drive · ~30% stroke / ~70% glide · lift-based foils | Rear prop as the story of how we move |
| Ray planform + slow high-aspect foils | Airplane wings on a fuselage as a fake ray |
| Computer holds a living hull (Board §01–02) | Sanding the form into a dart so it is passively pretty |

**Ridges** are a **tank coupon**, not the product and not a public performance claim. At hover / slow survey they are a weak bet. Do not brand them.

**Still law:** no robot-animal **product identity**. Look-alike follows what works (Board). That is not permission to ship a costume, and it is not permission to ship a generic AUV.

### 4. Multi-taxon

Leatherback is the first hull teacher. Rays (and later other lineages) earn their place when they teach a motion or planform we need. One animal is not the company.
