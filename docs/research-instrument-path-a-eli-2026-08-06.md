## 1. Executive map

**Field state**  
Bio-inspired underwater vehicles are active and crowded in propulsion morphologies (undulatory, oscillatory, pulsed-jet, soft fish). Most work optimises speed, manoeuvrability, or tank efficiency. Few treat **self-noise + long quiet loiter + ethical approach** as the primary design problem. Commercial small AUVs remain thruster-dominated and acoustically loud relative to ambient marine mammal soundscapes. Soft robots (MIT SoFi class and successors) have demonstrated real-ocean presence but remain short-endurance, control-heavy, and not yet optimised for multi-hour hydrophone-grade quiet.

**Whitespace**  
Honest co-presence design for a small boat-deployable platform: noise budget as first-class constraint, multi-mode quiet (hover / slow survey / efficient transit), surfaces that stay clean, and multi-taxon mechanism extraction under physics. Not another “efficient AUV” brochure.

**Top bets (mechanism level)**

1. Sparse / hybrid quiet thrust architecture (continuous prop only when needed)  
2. Shark-skin-inspired riblets sized to Path A Reynolds + fouling-aware design  
3. Multi-mode body + buoyancy for hover vs transit without mode conflict  
4. Sensor placement and isolation for hydrophone self-noise floor  
5. Soft or compliant outer layers only where they earn quiet or compliance (not as product identity)  
6. Volume-following / compressible ideas kept secondary (later depth)  
7. Foul-release + cleanable surface system as durability law  
8. Abort-on-avoidance behaviour as product law from day one

## 3. Innovation shortlist (ranked)

1. **Quiet multi-mode propulsion architecture**  
   Sparse or pulsed primary + very low continuous only when required.  
   *Lane:* Synthetic + Living lessons.  
   *First experiment:* tank noise + power comparison of candidate thrusters / undulatory modules at 0.3–1.5 m/s.  
   *Kill:* cannot beat a well-designed small prop on noise-at-useful-speed within Path A size/power.

2. **Riblet surface system sized to Path A**  
   Correct s+/h+ for expected Reynolds; foul-release or cleanable outer treatment.  
   *Lane:* Living.  
   *First experiment:* flat-plate and hull-section drag + fouling coupon tests in relevant water.  
   *Kill:* net drag increase or unmanageable fouling within one mission cycle.

3. **Hover / station-keep without continuous prop wash**  
   Buoyancy + paired fins or minimal sparse thrust.  
   *Lane:* Living / Synthetic.  
   *First experiment:* static and low-current hover power and noise.  
   *Kill:* hover power destroys multi-hour battery goal.

4. **Hydrophone-first vehicle layout**  
   Mechanical & flow isolation of sensors from residual propulsion and flow noise.  
   *Lane:* Synthetic engineering.  
   *First experiment:* self-noise spectra with hydrophone in candidate locations.  
   *Kill:* self-noise remains above useful marine-mammal / ambient bands during loiter.

5. **Cleanable / foul-aware outer skin**  
   Continuous or near-continuous surface preferred over deep open mosaic gaps for first builds.  
   *Lane:* Living lessons + Synthetic materials.  
   *First experiment:* fouling and cleanability of candidate skins after realistic immersion.  
   *Kill:* growth or cleaning labour makes multi-mission use impractical.

6. **Efficient slow transit (1–2 m/s) without race-car propulsion**  
   Form + sparse thrust + riblets.  
   *Lane:* Living + Synthetic.  
   *Kill:* cannot close distance usefully inside coastal visibility without raising noise floor.

7. **Volume-following compliance** (secondary)  
   Leatherback-inspired.  
   *Lane:* Living.  
   *First brief only:* mechanism extraction and scale analysis.  
   *Kill for Path A v1:* any complexity that delays quiet upper-water platform.

8. **Abort-on-avoidance behaviour layer**  
   Product law, not afterthought.  
   *Lane:* Ethics / Synthetic.  
   *Kill:* none — non-negotiable.

## 4. Path A composition recommendation

**Body**  
Streamlined rigid or semi-rigid core (makerspace printable / simple composite) sized 0.5–1.2 m. Soft or textured outer skin only where it earns quiet, drag, or cleanability. No open deep osteoderm gaps on first hull.

**Propulsion**  
Primary: quiet sparse or intermittent thrust + buoyancy for hover and slow survey.  
Secondary: efficient low-speed transit module (small prop or undulatory) used only when needed.  
Avoid continuous high-RPM prop as default.

**Surfaces**  
Shark-skin-inspired riblets only if regime-matched and paired with foul-release / cleanable treatment. Design for inspection and cleaning between missions.

**Buoyancy**  
Active or semi-active buoyancy for hover and depth-keeping inside 0–50 m (design toward 200 m). Glider lessons welcome; pure glider insufficient for the follow/observe job.

**Sensors**  
Hydrophone(s) placed and isolated for lowest practical self-noise during loiter. Stable (preferably steerable) camera. Layout serves listening first.

**Build sequence**

- **First:** sealed streamlined body + buoyancy + quietest available sparse thrust + hydrophone self-noise characterisation.  
- **Next:** surface treatment / riblets, refined hover, transit efficiency.  
- **Later:** undulatory modules, volume-following, deeper pressure-tolerant systems — only when earned.

Stay inside locked envelope. Do not reopen job card yet.

## 5. Ethics & welfare

- Approach and presence must reduce disturbance relative to typical small-boat + thruster methods.  
- Abort-on-avoidance is product law: if animals show clear avoidance or stress indicators, the platform withdraws or powers down propulsion.  
- No public claim of “we hang with whales” or demonstrated co-presence until evidence and welfare framing exist.  
- Norway / EU field work will require attention to local marine mammal disturbance guidelines and permitting; design for low impact from the start.  
- Form teachers are not the observation subjects; multi-taxon extraction does not justify close approaches that increase harm.
